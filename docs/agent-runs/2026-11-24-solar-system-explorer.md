## Implementation

- `src/astronomy.js` and `src/solar-view.js`: use offline heliocentric J2000 ecliptic vectors in AU, including Pluto, and a face-on orthographic scene on the existing Three.js renderer. The ruler applies to projected center-to-center separations on both axes; orbital height remains in the model but does not inflate the projection. Dots are deliberately enlarged. Labels yield to nearby dots and labels without moving the actual markers. [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-project-solar-system-at-one-au-scale.md) records the trade-offs.
- `index.html`, `styles.css`, and `src/app.js`: the nonmodal welcome can be dismissed by Explore or Escape. The native picker centers a body immediately, and the map keeps it centered as the clock advances. Choose Sun or Full system to return to the overview. The Inner planets preset, zoom buttons, wheel, and pinch vary the same scale. Starting Earth location selection, including the existing manual and globe-pin paths, does not reset playback. Without WebGL 2 the existing Earth location and demo actions open directly. The cheese module remains in source for the later issue, but is not initialized at startup.
- The Solar System scene shares WebGL resources at the renderer/context boundary, uses one circle geometry, ten markers and ten DOM labels, and does not request Earth/Moon atlas baking. There is one animation loop. Map draws at live speed are sampled at most once per simulated second; accelerated playback samples at most 20 times a real second. Zoom, resize, and paused date edits can repaint on the next frame. The existing observer-free globe preview stays demand-painted.
